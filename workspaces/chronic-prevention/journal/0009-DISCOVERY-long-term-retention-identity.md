---
name: long-term-retention-identity-not-motivation
description: Long-term users don't sustain engagement because they're motivated — they sustain because the product becomes part of their identity. Measure identity markers (self-description, community membership, daily routine integration) not just engagement metrics
type: DISCOVERY
---

**Finding:** Evidence from Whoop, Omada, and Calm all converge: users who sustain at 6+ months do so because the product becomes part of how they see themselves ("I'm someone who tracks my recovery") not because they're motivated to avoid disease. Motivation fluctuates daily. Identity persists across motivation troughs.

**Why this is non-obvious:** Product managers optimize for engagement metrics (Day-30 retention, recommendation completion rate). These are leading indicators, not the thing itself. The thing itself is whether a user introduces the product to a friend, describes it as part of their routine, or reacts to a suggestion that it might not work for them.

**Measure at 90 days:** "Would you recommend this to a friend?" (NPS) and "How would you describe Metabo to someone who asked what it is?" (identity marker). Users who give a specific identity-consistent description ("I use it to track my metabolic health and see patterns before I get sick") have higher 6-month retention than users who describe it functionally ("it's a health app").
