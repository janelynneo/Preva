**Finding:** Three independent research streams converge on sleep as the highest-leverage intervention in metabolic disease prevention:

1. **Epidemiology:** Sleep < 6 hours is associated with 30% increased T2D risk (NHANES analysis); sleep deprivation directly impairs insulin sensitivity through cortisol and inflammatory pathways.
2. **Behaviour:** Singapore desk workers average 6.5–7 hours of sleep on weeknights — already in the elevated-risk band. Improving to 7.5–8 hours is a concrete, achievable target.
3. **ML signal:** HRV and sleep efficiency are the two most reliable wearable-derived features for anomaly detection — they update daily and correlate with metabolic trajectory.

**The compounding effect:** Sleep is not just one input to metabolic health — it affects every other behavior the product wants to influence. Poor sleep → reduced willpower → worse food choices → lower exercise motivation → elevated stress → worse sleep. Breaking this cycle at the sleep node has disproportionate leverage.

**Implication for product:** Sleep quality/duration should be the primary daily check-in variable (not steps, not food), and the core daily recommendation should often be sleep-adjacent ("go to bed 30 minutes earlier," "no screens after 11pm," "your deep sleep was 12% below baseline this week").
